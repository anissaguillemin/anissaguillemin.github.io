Anissa Guillemin.

Theoretical Biology

Investigating fundamental questions in biology.

My research spans multiple scales of biological organisation, from gene expression dynamics, cell-to-cell molecular variability and transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulation to patient treatment trajectories.

I investigate these processes in various biological and clinical contexts, including health, infection, cancer and therapeutic interventions. To address these questions, I combine experimental approaches, mathematical modelling, bioinformatics, statistical analysis and clinical research methodology.

01 / Research interest

01

Cellular heterogeneity

I find it fascinating that genetically identical cells exposed to similar conditions can behave so differently. Understanding the origins, dynamics and functional consequences of this heterogeneity is a question that has accompanied me throughout my scientific career.

02

Systems biology

I am fascinated by how complex biological behaviours emerge from relatively simple molecular interactions. I particularly enjoy using mathematical models to challenge biological assumptions and explore the interplay between randomness and determinism in living systems.

03

Gene expression & regulation

I am intrigued by the multiple layers of regulation that connect the genome to cellular phenotypes. I enjoy investigating how cells adjust gene expression in response to environmental changes, stress and infection, and how these mechanisms contribute to cellular diversity.

04

Biostatistics

What I particularly enjoy about statistics is the process of translating a biological question into a quantitative framework. I like exploring data, questioning apparent patterns and assessing whether they reflect meaningful biological phenomena or arise from variability and uncertainty.

05

Clinical research

I find the diversity of individual patient trajectories particularly intriguing. I enjoy investigating how biological, pharmacological and individual factors interact to shape treatment outcomes, while contributing to research that can ultimately improve patient care.

06

Machine learning

Beyond prediction, I am particularly interested in the ability of machine learning to uncover unexpected biological relationships. What excites me most is understanding why a model makes a prediction and using its findings to formulate new, testable biological hypotheses.

02 / Experience

2024 – Present

Clinical Research Project Manager / Study Coordinator

Clinical Oncology Pharmacy Unit · Hospices Civils de Lyon · France

Under the supervision of Prof. Catherine Rioufol, I investigate patient treatment trajectories in oncology, with the objective of better understanding the determinants of treatment tolerability, adherence, relative dose intensity and patient-reported outcomes. I design clinical study protocols and statistical analysis plans, develop reproducible analytical workflows in R, and coordinate prospective observational studies. This position has allowed me to extend my quantitative background to clinical research while developing skills in research project management, scientific supervision and multidisciplinary collaboration.

2021 – 2024

Postdoctoral researcher

RNA Metabolism in Immunity and Infection · Laboratory of Biology and Modeling of the Cell · France

With Dr. Emiliano Ricci, I investigated how post-transcriptional and translational regulation of gene expression contributes to innate immune and antiviral responses, with a particular focus on interferon-mediated changes in ribosomes and their interacting partners. I combined molecular biology experiments with transcriptomic and proteomic data analyses to characterise the mechanisms underlying these cellular responses. During this postdoctoral experience, I developed my expertise in RNA biology, gene expression regulation and the analysis of complex molecular datasets.

2019 – 2021

Postdoctoral researcher

Theoretical Systems Biology · Melbourne Integrative Genomics · University of Melbourne · Australia

With Prof. Michael P. H. Stumpf, I investigated the theoretical principles underlying cell-fate decisions, aiming to understand how gene expression dynamics and molecular fluctuations shape cellular trajectories within epigenetic landscapes. I developed deterministic and stochastic mathematical models using ordinary and stochastic differential equations, drawing on dynamical systems theory and non-equilibrium statistical physics. This experience strengthened my expertise in mathematical modelling, theoretical biology and scientific computing in Julia.

2015 – 2018

PhD in Biology

Systems Biology of Decision Making · Laboratory of Biology and Modeling of the Cell · France

Under the supervision of Dr. Olivier Gandrillon, I investigated the role of stochastic gene expression variability in cell differentiation and cell-fate decisions, using avian erythropoiesis as a biological model. My objective was to understand whether molecular fluctuations were merely a consequence of cellular differentiation or could actively contribute to driving this process. To address this question, I combined single-cell gene expression measurements, pharmacological perturbations, statistical analyses and mathematical modelling, developing an interdisciplinary approach at the interface between experimental and theoretical biology.

Scientific outreach

I believe that sharing scientific knowledge beyond the academic community is an important part of being a researcher. I particularly enjoy making complex biological concepts accessible to non-specialist audiences, stimulating scientific curiosity and encouraging critical thinking. For me, communicating science is not only about sharing knowledge, but also about inspiring people to ask their own questions.

Since 2016, I have been actively involved in various science outreach initiatives, both as an organiser and a speaker, including Pint of Science, Pop'Sciences and the French national science festival (Fête de la Science). Through my involvement in the DéMesures association, including a leadership role, I have organised scientific events, contributed to educational activities in schools and collaborated with the Musée des Confluences in Lyon. I have also participated in initiatives aimed at introducing younger generations to scientific research, such as Déclics and the AFM-Téléthon's 1000 chercheurs dans les écoles programme, sharing my research experience and scientific career path with students.
